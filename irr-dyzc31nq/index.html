The have-tos · Phoenix Valley home repairs

The have-tos that hold up Valley closings.

These are the issues Phoenix-area inspectors report most often that matter to lenders, appraisers and buyers. Desert heat, monsoon storms, hard water and Valley soils wear homes in specific ways. Below, they are grouped into three have-to categories.[1–6]

1

Safety

Hazards to people. Buyers, lenders and insurers expect these fixed first.

Electrical hazards

  • Missing or failed GFCI protection in kitchens, baths, garage, laundry and outdoors
  • Recalled or problem panels (Federal Pacific Stab-Lok, Zinsco), double-tapped breakers
  • Aluminum branch wiring in some older homes; open grounds, reversed polarity, exposed splices

Life-safety items

  • Smoke and carbon monoxide detectors missing or not working
  • Water heater TPR (temperature-pressure relief) valve or discharge line missing or faulty
  • Loose, missing or low stair railings and guardrails; garage-door auto-reverse

Pool barrier when the pool is part of the sale

  • Barrier fencing, gates, self-closing and self-latching hardware under state and city pool rules
  • Door alarms and openings that let children reach the pool area
2

Structure

Safety, structure, and function.

The bones of the house: foundation, framing, roof, and the wood-destroying insects that attack it.

Foundation

  • Slab-on-grade foundations over expansive clay, collapsible soils or caliche that move with monsoon wet-dry cycles
  • Grading and drainage that sends water toward the slab. This is the most common house problem on InterNACHI's list.
  • When there are signs of active movement, the next step is a licensed structural engineer

Structure & stucco

  • Diagonal cracks at door and window corners, stair-step cracks in block walls, doors that stick
  • Hairline stucco cracks are usually cosmetic. Wide, growing or patterned cracks get a closer look.
  • Termite and wood-destroying-insect damage (a WDI report is routine in the Valley and many lenders require one)

Roof

  • Concrete or clay tile: the tile lasts decades, but the underlayment beneath it often fails first
  • Flat and foam roofs: blistering, cracking, ponding, seams, coatings due for a recoat
  • Monsoon damage: slipped or broken tiles, flashing, blocked drainage, active leaks
3

Function & systems

The systems a house needs to work every day, especially through a Phoenix summer.

Plumbing

  • Polybutylene supply pipe (common in late-1970s to mid-1990s homes) and aging galvanized pipe
  • Active leaks at sinks and toilets, failed shower valves, slab leaks
  • Hard-water scale damaging fixtures, valves, supply lines and water heaters

Heating & cooling

  • AC is the most expensive system in a Phoenix home and often the first to fail after years of 110°F summers
  • Age, tonnage, refrigerant and service history; rooftop package units and gas packs
  • Non-cooling units, failed heat strips or furnaces, damaged ducts

Water heater

  • Hard water shortens tank life: sediment, corrosion, leaking tanks
  • Venting, gas connection, strapping and drain-pan issues

Windows & pool equipment

  • Broken or fogged double-pane windows. Fogging between the panes means the seal has failed and the glass unit needs replacing.
  • Pool equipment, when the pool is part of the sale: pump, filter, heater, leaks and electrical bonding
